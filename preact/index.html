An Open-System Model

Both equations below are derived from conservation of total exchangeable sodium (\(\text{TES} = \text{Na}_{\text{start}} \times \text{TBW}\)), with explicit tracking of sodium and water gained from the infusate and lost in urine over time \(T\). Urinary potassium is included because K⁺ excretion is osmotically equivalent to Na⁺ retention and therefore raises serum sodium in the same way.

Equation 1 — Required IVF Rate to Reach a Target Sodium

$$IVFRate = \frac{\text{Na}_{end} \cdot TBW - \text{Na}_{end} \cdot T \cdot U_{rate} - \text{Na}_{start} \cdot TBW + T \cdot (U_{Na} + U_K) \cdot U_{rate}} {T \cdot (C_{IVF} - \text{Na}_{end})}$$

Equation 2 — Predicted Serum Sodium After a Planned Infusion

$$\text{Na}_{end} = \frac{C_{IVF} \cdot IVFRate \cdot T + \text{Na}_{start} \cdot TBW - T \cdot (U_{Na} + U_K) \cdot U_{rate}} {IVFRate \cdot T + TBW - T \cdot U_{rate}}$$

Setting \(U_{rate} = 0\) (anuric patient) reduces both equations to the Adrogué-Madias closed-system result.

Variables
